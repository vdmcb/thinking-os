# Meridian Field Services — FY27 Platform Expansion Business Case

## Executive summary

Meridian proposes replacing its dispatch tooling with the Atlas platform across all regions in FY27. The initiative requires $2.4M in FY27 spend and is justified by an expected 22% reduction in cost per completed work order by Q4 FY27. Industry adoption of AI-assisted dispatch is accelerating, and Meridian risks a competitive gap if it does not modernize this cycle.

## Current state

Meridian operates 14 regional dispatch centers handling 410,000 work orders annually. Average cost per completed work order is $61 (FY26 actual, finance system of record). Dispatcher headcount is 122. Legacy tooling (RouteMaster 9) is on extended support until March 2029. Annual RouteMaster licensing is $310,000. Three regions run a customized RouteMaster build with local integrations.

## The Atlas pilot

A 90-day pilot ran in the Southwest region between April and June FY26. The pilot covered 21,400 work orders — 5.2% of annual volume. Cost per completed work order in the pilot region fell from $63 to $49, a 22% reduction. The Southwest region was selected because its dispatch team had the highest tooling-adoption scores in the FY25 internal survey. During the pilot window, the region also onboarded two large commercial contracts that shifted its job mix toward higher-density urban routes.

## Financial model

The model applies the pilot's 22% unit-cost reduction to all 410,000 annual work orders, yielding $5.5M gross annual savings. Net of $2.4M FY27 costs and $800,000 ongoing Atlas licensing, the case projects $2.3M net benefit in FY27 and $4.7M annually thereafter. The model assumes full deployment by end of Q2 FY27, all 14 regions reaching pilot-level performance within one quarter of go-live, and no attrition-driven backfill costs during transition.

## Implementation plan

Phase 1 (Q1): core platform deployment, 4 regions. Phase 2 (Q2): remaining 10 regions, including the three customized-integration regions. The integration effort for the customized regions has not been separately estimated; the plan allocates a shared $150,000 contingency across all workstreams. The vendor's professional-services team leads deployment; Meridian's IT contributes two engineers part-time.

## Vendor assessment

Atlas was selected over two alternatives, Fieldly and DispatchPro. Atlas scored highest on a weighted matrix covering functionality, integration effort, and total cost. Fieldly's total-cost cell was populated from a 2024 analyst report; DispatchPro declined to provide reference customers, and its integration-effort cell is marked "estimated internally." Atlas's figures come from its own proposal.

## Risk register

Twelve risks are tracked. Top three: adoption lag in low-scoring regions (mitigation: training program, owner: HR); integration overrun in customized regions (mitigation: contingency, owner: IT); vendor delivery slippage (mitigation: contractual milestones, owner: Procurement). The register lists likelihoods and impacts as High/Medium/Low without definitions.

## Organizational readiness

A change council meets biweekly with representation from Operations, IT, Finance, and HR. Regional VPs receive monthly dashboards. The council reviews adoption metrics and may escalate to the COO. The business case does not state who is accountable for the 22% target itself, who may pause rollout if pilot-level performance does not materialize, or what threshold would trigger such a decision.

## Timeline sensitivities

Atlas releases its v12 platform in Q3 FY27; deploying on v11 requires a mid-year upgrade. RouteMaster support ends March 2029, leaving two full budget cycles of fallback. Procurement notes Atlas pricing is quoted through December FY26 only.
