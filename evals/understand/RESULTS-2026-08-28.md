Five private documents, run with the 2.1.0 skill as merged in PR #3 plus the three-round audit cap from this change. Sources, outputs, and audit transcripts are not committed; they live in `evals/understand/private/` (gitignored) on the machine that ran the trial. Scores are model-judged by the operator of the run, not a human usefulness session; rubric dimension 14 (run and follow-up) is therefore unscored.

## How the runs went

- D1, D2, D4: run by subagents executing SKILL.md 2.1.0 as committed in PR #3. Each subagent's own audit loop did not converge before the runs were cut off by account spend limits: D1 reached its 27th draft, D4 its ninth audit round, D2 its 16th draft. The latest draft of each was taken as the run output and given fresh, independent audits under the three-round cap.
- D3: run in-session by the operator after its subagent left no output; audited under the cap.
- D5: run in-session; three audit rounds.
- The uncapped loop is the reason for the cap in this change. A strict auditor ("prefer a false alarm") always finds one more qualifier; without a stop rule the loop consumes the budget without a final output.

That is a skill gap, not a document gap, and is the first follow-up below.

## Follow-ups this trial produced

1. Locators for sources without pages: reading.md should name the fallback (section heading, table caption, or ordinal paragraph) so HTML and Markdown outputs can meet the traceability minimum.
2. Clipped sources: the "quote only intact text" rule held once applied, but the subagent reconstructed ten clipped words before the audit caught them. The rule may need to sit in step 3 (ledger) as well as step 2, so the ledger records the cut.
3. Length cuts are a fault source of their own: two of the D4 and D5 findings were silences overstated by trimming words to meet the ceiling. The compression pass in output-format.md should say that a cut which removes a qualifier is a fact change and re-enters the audit.
