# Reference: Northstar fulfilment capacity decision

## In one sentence

The source asks for $2.4 million and a five-person team for an automated packing cell, but its preferred-option claim rests on alternatives compared with different cost boundaries, evidence statuses, and missing values rather than a common decision basis.

## Required comparison handling

- Preserve the decision request without recommending any option.
- Reconstruct the source's selection logic: `automated cell → higher throughput, lower long-run cost, and less disruption than alternatives → authorize the investment`.
- Identify the comparison as incomplete and asymmetric: overflow throughput is absent; the repair, lease, and build costs include different components; no common period or total-cost boundary is supplied; and disruption labels are undefined.
- Keep the four-week measured current-line throughput separate from the automated-cell vendor target. A target observed at a demonstration facility is not an actual result for this warehouse or parcel mix.
- Do not infer a ranking, normalize costs with invented assumptions, or treat a populated comparison table as evidence that the options were assessed on equivalent scopes.

## Required evidence and unknowns

- The contractor quote and vendor target are source-provided evidence with stated exclusions or applicability limits, not independently verified proof of the preferred option.
- The `lowest long-run cost` claim cannot be checked without a common analysis period, included cost categories, residual value, and operating assumptions.
- The `least operational disruption` claim cannot be checked without a definition, measurement method, or evidence for each option.
- The preferred option's throughput advantage cannot be interpreted without the demand forecast and applicability bridge from the demonstration facility.

## Material pushback

Request a like-for-like decision matrix that shows, for every option, the same demand scenario, analysis period, throughput definition, included and excluded capital and operating costs, disruption metric, evidence provenance, and weighting or selection rule. Require missing cells to remain unknown rather than scored. Explain that this is needed to determine whether the source's preferred-option claim follows from comparable evidence rather than unevenly specified alternatives.
