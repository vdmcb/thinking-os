# Reference: Meridian workflow service launch case

## In one sentence

The source requests $840,000 and a seven-person unit for 12 months, and uses an external survey about pricing preference and budget concern to support customer, retention, and margin forecasts that the survey does not measure.

## Required evidence-boundary handling

- Treat the 72% and 64% results as externally cited evidence, unverified, about stated pricing preference and concern in the surveyed population.
- Do not treat stated preference as observed willingness to pay, purchase, retention, expansion, or delivery economics.
- Identify the surveyed large North American companies as a population mismatch for the claimed Nordic mid-market manufacturing market unless the source supplies an applicability bridge.
- Keep 15 signed customers, 125% net revenue retention, and 65% gross margin classified as forecasts or model outputs, not survey findings or actual performance.
- Trace the inferential jumps separately: `preference → willingness to pay → purchase/conversion → renewal and expansion → revenue and margin`. Evidence for one hop must not be allowed to validate the later hops.

## Required reasoning

- Reconstruct the source's chain as `fund unit → offer fixed-base-plus-usage package → acquire customers → retain and expand accounts → deliver at the assumed cost → achieve the forecast economics`.
- Mark the survey as relevant only to the package-preference proposition; it does not establish purchase, retention, expansion, or margin.
- Preserve that the citation was not independently checked.

## Material pushback

Request an evidence-to-claim bridge that maps each forecast premise to an inspectable artifact: target-market willingness-to-pay evidence at the proposed price, funnel assumptions or observed conversion data, cohort evidence for renewal and expansion, and a delivery cost model for gross margin. Explain that this determines which commercial outcomes, if any, the preference evidence can support.
