# Writing for a human reader

Every Thinking OS skill exists because a human is carrying a burden they cannot discharge by reading the source. Output that costs as much attention as the source has failed regardless of how faithful it is. These rules bind every skill's output.

## Reader burden is the binding constraint

1. **The output must cost materially less to read than the source.**
2. **Ranking is part of faithfulness.** Findings, facts, and steps are not equally consequential. Giving a minor one the same space as a decisive one misrepresents the source.
3. **Compression is not deletion.** Cut elaboration, merge duplicates, hold detail for a follow-up. Never drop a caveat, a number, a conflict, or a "the source does not say".

## Budgets follow ideas, not pages

A budget is a ceiling on how much the reader must hold, so it is set by the number of ideas the output asks them to hold, never by the length of the source. A fifty-page deck making one argument is a simple source. Each skill defines its own idea unit and the ceiling per tier; the lint checks structure first and words second. When a material fact and the ceiling genuinely conflict, keep the fact, cut elsewhere, and say in one line that the budget was exceeded for it.

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs three commas, split it.
- Plain verbs: "is", "has", "shows", "sends", "keeps". Not "serves as", "facilitates", "showcases", "orchestrates".
- Active voice with a named actor: "the model applies the pilot rate to all regions", not "the rate is applied".
- No forced groups of three.
- No "not X but Y" scaffolding. State Y.
- No dramatic fragments, no reveals staged with a colon.

## Words

Do not use: delve, pivotal, crucial, testament, tapestry, showcase, boasts, landscape (figurative), foster, vibrant, breathtaking, nestled, renowned, seamless, robust (figurative), holistic, leverage (as a verb), utilize, underscore, elevate, orchestrate, facilitate, paradigm, ecosystem (figurative).

Do not use filler: "in order to" (write "to"), "due to the fact that" (write "because"), "at this point in time" (write "now"), "it is worth noting that" (just note it), "it is important to note" (same), "basically", "essentially", "simply put".

## Patterns from live review

- No document personification. A deck does not count, believe, assume, want, or hope. Write what it presents, shows, states, or fails to show.
- No inherited shorthand. The source's internal vocabulary gets one plain introduction before any use, or gets replaced with a description. The reader may never open the source.
- No confirmations of the expected. Passed checks and things working as they should earn no words.
- No preamble that announces content ("Let's look at", "Here's what you need to know"). State the fact.
- No fake-depth openers ("At its core", "The real question is", "The key insight is").
- No heading restated as the first sentence under it.
- No generic endings ("The future looks bright", "Now you know"). End on the last concrete fact.
- No defending against objections nobody raised.

## Typography

- Never em-dashes or en-dashes, including in ranges: write "3-5". Use a comma, colon, period, or parentheses where a dash would go.
- No emoji and no decorative symbols.
- Bold and italics only where they carry information the reader needs to find again. Never for emphasis.
- Straight quotes. Sentence case in headings.

## Tone

- No chatbot residue: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Great question", "Certainly".
- No hedging stacks: "might" or "could", never "could potentially possibly".
- No praise of the source and no scorn for it. The output reports; the reader judges.

## The read-aloud test

Before returning, read one paragraph aloud (or simulate it). If a sentence would make a colleague pause, rewrite it. If a phrase could appear unchanged in output about any other source, it is carrying no information; cut it.
