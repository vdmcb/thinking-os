# Human voice

The explanation must read as if a careful colleague explained the file out loud to someone new. Style noise is cognitive load. These rules are informed by Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" and the blader/humanizer pattern set, adapted to plain explanations.

Apply them while writing and again in the repeat-back pass. They never justify dropping a fact, a number, or a "the file does not say".

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. Under fifteen words when you can. Never over twenty-five.
- Plain verbs. "is", "has", "sends", "keeps", "drops". Not "serves as", "facilitates", "orchestrates".
- Active voice with a named actor: "the client sends the request", not "the request is sent".
- No forced groups of three.
- No "not X but Y" scaffolding. State Y.
- No dramatic fragments.

## Words

Do not use: delve, pivotal, crucial, testament, tapestry, showcase, boasts, landscape (figurative), foster, vibrant, seamless, robust (figurative), holistic, leverage (as a verb), utilize, underscore, elevate, orchestrate, facilitate, paradigm, ecosystem (figurative).

Do not use filler: "in order to" (write "to"), "due to the fact that" (write "because"), "at this point in time" (write "now"), "it is worth noting that" (just note it), "it is important to note" (same), "basically", "essentially", "simply put".

When a technical word is unavoidable because the reader will meet it in the file, introduce it once with a concrete picture. Otherwise replace it with what it does.

## No baby talk

The reader has no background. They are not a child.

- No exclamation marks.
- No pet names: "kiddo", "buddy", "little one".
- No "imagine you have", "picture this", "think of it like a magic box".
- No story frames: "once upon a time", "let's go on a journey".
- No rhetorical questions: "so what happens next?"
- No cheer: "cool, right?", "isn't that neat", "great job".
- No "so basically".

## Structure

- No preamble that announces content ("Let me explain", "Here's the simple version"). State the fact.
- No fake-depth openers ("At its core", "The key insight is").
- No heading restated as the first sentence under it.
- No generic endings ("And that's it!", "Now you know"). End on the last fact.
- Sentence case in headings.

## Typography

- Never em-dashes or en-dashes, including in ranges. Write "3-5". Use a comma, colon, period, or parentheses where a dash would go.
- No emoji, no decorative symbols.
- Bold only in the status line.
- Straight quotes.

## Tone

- No chatbot residue: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Great question", "Certainly".
- No hedging stacks: "might" or "could", never "could potentially possibly".
- No praise of the file and no scorn for it.

## The repeat-back test

Read one section aloud, or simulate it. Could a colleague with no background say it back in their own words after hearing it once? If they would pause on a word, replace the word. If they would pause on a sentence, split it. If they would ask "wait, what is that?", a term was used before its introduction.
